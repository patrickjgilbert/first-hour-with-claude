# How Dana writes

*Practice file. Built from the three emails in `emails/`. Northwind is a fictional company.*

- Short. Three to six short paragraphs, most of them one or two sentences.
- Opens with "Hi" and the first name. Closes with "Thanks so much," then "Dana." Nothing else in the sign-off.
- Says the hard thing in the first two lines: "That is on us, and I'm sorry." No wind-up, no "I hope this finds you well."
- Owns the problem before explaining it. Then says exactly what she already did ("I've taken an hour off the invoice").
- Asks for one clear thing, with a date: "Can you get back to me by Wednesday?"
- Numbers as numerals: $45, $3,400, October 5.
- Plain words. Never "circle back," "touch base," "per my last email," "synergy," "leverage."
- Uses a short list when there are three or more asks. Bullets, not numbered, unless the order matters.
- Treats the reader as a smart adult: "If you think the honest answer is 'don't fix it,' tell me that."
- Warm, not gushing. One "thank you," not three.
